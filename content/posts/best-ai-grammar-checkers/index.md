AI grammar checkers have moved beyond simple spell-check. The best ones now analyze tone, suggest rewrites, detect plagiarism, and integrate into your workflow across browsers, editors, and email clients. But the gap between free and paid features is significant, and choosing the wrong tool means either paying for features you do not need or missing errors that matter.

This article compares the four major grammar checking tools based on verified pricing, feature differences, and practical use cases.

## Quick Comparison

| Tool | Free Tier | Paid Price | Best For |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|
| Grammarly Pro | Yes (grammar + spelling + 100 AI prompts) | $12/mo (annual) / $30/mo | All-purpose writing |
| ProWritingAid | Yes (500 word limit) | $10/mo (annual) / $399 lifetime | Long-form writing, authors |
| LanguageTool | Yes (basic rules) | ~$5/mo (annual) | Multi-language, privacy |
| QuillBot | Yes (limited paraphrasing) | $10/mo (annual) | Paraphrasing and rewriting |

## Grammarly Pro (formerly Premium)

Grammarly is the most widely used grammar checker. In March 2025, Grammarly renamed its Premium plan to Pro ([Grammarly Pro page](https://www.grammarly.com/pro)).

**Verified pricing:**

| Plan | Annual Billing | Monthly Billing |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Free | $0 | $0 |
| Pro | $12/user/month | $30/user/month |
| Business | $15-25/user/month | Quote-based |

**Free tier covers:**
- Grammar and spelling correction
- Basic punctuation suggestions
- 100 monthly AI prompts
- Browser extension, desktop app, mobile keyboard

**Pro adds:**
- Full-sentence rewrites
- Tone detection and adjustment
- Clarity and conciseness suggestions
- Plagiarism detection
- Advanced style and delivery feedback
- Up to 149 seats for team use

**What Grammarly Pro does well:**
- Catches the widest range of errors across grammar, punctuation, and style
- Browser extension works in Gmail, Google Docs, Word, social media, and most web text fields
- Explanations for suggestions help writers learn from corrections
- Tone detection is useful for professional communication

**Known limitations:**
- Pro suggestions can feel prescriptive for writers with an intentional style
- Occasionally flags correct but uncommon phrasing
- Can slow down browser performance when checking long documents
- Plagiarism checker requires Pro plan; free tier does not include it

**When Grammarly Pro is worth it:** You write professionally (emails, reports, content) and need consistent error checking across all your applications. The $12/month annual price is reasonable for daily use.

## ProWritingAid

ProWritingAid targets writers who need deeper analysis than basic grammar checking. It provides 20+ writing analysis reports covering pacing, repetition, readability, dialogue tags, and more.

**Verified pricing** ([ProWritingAid Pricing](https://prowritingaid.com/pricing), [SaaS Price Pulse](https://www.saaspricepulse.com/tools/prowritingaid)):

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Lifetime |
|------|---------|--------|----------|
| Free | $0 | $0 | — |
| Premium | $30/mo | $120/yr ($10/mo) | ~$399 |
| Premium Pro | — | $144/yr ($12/mo) | ~$699 |

The lifetime option is unusual in this market and offers significant savings for long-term users. ProWritingAid [confirms that lifetime subscriptions include all future updates](https://help.prowritingaid.com/article/389-what-does-a-lifetime-subscription-mean).

**What ProWritingAid does well:**
- Writing style analysis goes deeper than any competitor
- Reports on overused words, sentence length variation, readability metrics
- Integrates with Scrivener, Word, Google Docs, and desktop apps
- Lifetime plan eliminates recurring subscription cost

**Known limitations:**
- Free tier limited to 500 words per check — too restrictive for real use
- Interface is less polished than Grammarly
- The number of reports can be overwhelming for casual users
- Not as seamless in-browser as Grammarly (fewer integrations)

**When ProWritingAid is worth it:** You write long-form content (books, academic papers, long articles) and benefit from structural analysis beyond grammar. The lifetime plan at $399 is a good deal if you write regularly and want to avoid monthly subscriptions.

## LanguageTool

LanguageTool is an open-source grammar checker with strong multilingual support and a self-hosting option.

**Key differentiator: Open-source and self-hostable.**

LanguageTool can run on your own server with no request limits. However, the self-hosted version only includes free-tier grammar rules — it does not include the premium rules (advanced style and punctuation checks) without a premium license ([Stack Overflow discussion](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/66631529/is-languagetool-free-if-i-host-my-own-server)).

**Verified pricing:**

| Plan | Price |
|------|-------|
| Free (cloud) | $0, basic rules |
| Free (self-hosted) | $0, basic rules, unlimited requests |
| Premium (cloud) | ~$5/month (annual billing) |
| Teams/Business | Higher, quote-based |

**What LanguageTool does well:**
- Supports 30+ languages (best multilingual grammar checker)
- Open-source code is transparent and auditable
- Self-hosting option for complete data privacy
- More affordable than Grammarly for paid features

**Known limitations:**
- English grammar checking is less accurate than Grammarly
- Fewer integrations and smaller extension ecosystem
- Self-hosted version lacks premium rules without additional licensing
- Interface is functional but less polished

**Self-hosting costs:** If you want to self-host LanguageTool with reasonable performance, hosting costs start around $18-33/month for a basic server ([Elestio pricing](https://elest.io/open-source/languagetool/resources/plans-and-pricing)). This is more expensive than the cloud premium plan unless you need the privacy guarantee.

**When LanguageTool is worth it:** You write in multiple languages, you need data privacy (self-hosting), or you want the most affordable paid grammar checker at ~$5/month.

## QuillBot

QuillBot focuses on paraphrasing and rewriting rather than comprehensive grammar checking.

**Verified pricing:**

| Plan | Price |
|------|-------|
| Free | Limited paraphrasing, basic grammar |
| Premium | $10/month (annual billing) |

**What QuillBot does well:**
- Paraphrasing with multiple tone modes (Standard, Formal, Creative, Fluency)
- Useful for breaking writer's block or finding alternative phrasing
- Includes a summarizer and citation generator

**Known limitations:**
- Paraphrasing can subtly change meaning — always review the output
- Grammar checking is less comprehensive than Grammarly or ProWritingAid
- Free tier limits paraphrasing to short passages
- Not a complete grammar solution on its own

**When QuillBot is worth it:** You primarily need help rephrasing and rewriting sentences, not comprehensive grammar checking. Pairs well with Grammarly Free for a complete setup.

## How to Choose

| Your Situation | Best Tool | Why |
|---------------|-----------|-----|
| Professional writing daily | Grammarly Pro | Most comprehensive, works everywhere |
| Writing books or long articles | ProWritingAid Premium | Deep style analysis, lifetime option |
| Writing in multiple languages | LanguageTool Premium | Best multilingual support |
| Need data privacy | LanguageTool (self-hosted) | Open-source, runs locally |
| Budget under $10/month | LanguageTool Premium (~$5/mo) | Good value for paid features |
| Need paraphrasing help | QuillBot | Best rewriting tool |
| Want free only | Grammarly Free + QuillBot Free | Covers grammar and basic rewriting |

## The Practical Stack

**For most people:** Grammarly Free covers basic grammar and spelling in your browser. Upgrade to Pro ($12/month) when you need tone detection and full-sentence rewrites for professional communication.

**For authors:** ProWritingAid for structural analysis and style reports. Pair with Grammarly Free for quick in-browser checking.

**For privacy-conscious users or multilingual writers:** LanguageTool Premium at ~$5/month. Self-host if data cannot leave your infrastructure.

## FAQ

### Is Grammarly Pro worth $12/month?
If you write professionally (emails, reports, content marketing, client communication), yes. The tone detection, full-sentence rewrites, and plagiarism detection are the features that justify the cost. For casual use, the free tier is sufficient.

### Can these tools replace a human editor?
For everyday writing (emails, internal documents, social media), they are sufficient. For published books, legal documents, academic papers, or content where brand voice precision matters, human editing still provides value that AI grammar checkers cannot match.

### Is Grammarly safe for confidential documents?
Grammarly processes your text on their servers to provide corrections. Their [privacy policy](https://www.grammarly.com/privacy-policy) states they do not sell user data, but if you work with highly confidential content (legal, medical, proprietary), consider LanguageTool self-hosted where text never leaves your machine.

### Which is more accurate for English writing?
Grammarly consistently catches the most errors and provides the most helpful suggestions for English writing. ProWritingAid is close for grammar but excels in style analysis for long-form content.
